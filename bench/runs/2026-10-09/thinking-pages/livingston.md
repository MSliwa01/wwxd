# How Jessica Livingston thinks

## What they optimize for
- **Making something users want.** Users are the only judges that count, and they care about the product, not your credentials. Everything else follows from the product (said in both sources). See [[startups/fundamentals/playing-house-and-gaming-the-system]].
  - "focus on making something people want everything follows from that" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 24:36; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "in a start-up it's the users who judge you and they don't care about your credentials they care about your product" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 25:45; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "the hard stuff is building something people want" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 14:03; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **More startups in the world, not more money for YC.** She says YC was built to help people start startups, which VCs were not doing (said in both sources). See [[fundraising/investors/how-yc-was-designed-differently-from-vcs]].
  - "we were not driven by money we were interested in startups and we wanted to help people start more of them" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 18:52; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "the whole reason we did this was to try to cause try to help Young startups but cause there to be more startups to be created" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 41:03; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "VCS were not in the business of encouraging more people to start startups" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 3:58; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Earnest people and an unpretentious culture.** She funded only earnest people early on and says that choice is the basis of the alumni culture (said in both sources). See [[fundraising/investors/what-yc-looks-for-in-founders]].
  - "from the beginning I was very careful about only funding earnest people" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 14:51; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "early on I was pretty rigid about this and I think that's the basis of the culture of our alumni community" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 15:27; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "Earnest and not pretentious that's in YC's DNA" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 45:34; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Community built by bringing people together.** She treats events as the core of YC because batches make everything an event and good things happen when people meet (said in both sources). See [[fundraising/investors/events-and-founder-community]].
  - "events are a crucial part of what YC does when you fund startups in batches everything's an event" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 17:10; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "anytime you can bring people together with an event good things always happen" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 7:21; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "this batch thing is even more special because people became friends" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 20:57; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Making it simple and easy to start.** She wanted to remove complexity like legal paperwork so founders could focus on the hard part. See [[fundraising/investors/how-yc-was-designed-differently-from-vcs]].
  - "we wanted to make it easy for young technical people to start a startup" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 14:03; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "we thought let's just standardize this let's just make it simple and easy" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 15:13; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "a standard deal just takes so much complexity out of the equation" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 16:22; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Founders' confidence.** She says belief from others is something founders badly need, and YC supplies it. See [[fundraising/investors/events-and-founder-community]].
  - "confidence is a huge thing that YC brings to the table" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 12:23; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "if you have no one who believes in you gosh it's sort of depressing and demoralizing" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 12:56; by: livingston; conf: high)

## Rules they decide by
- **Judge the founders' character and their relationship, not the idea.** She looks for earnestness, determination and flexibility, and above all at how the co-founders get along (said in both sources). See [[fundraising/investors/what-yc-looks-for-in-founders]].
  - "did they seem earnest did they seem determined were they flexible minded and most importantly what was the relationship between the co-founders like" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 14:51; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "it's about the founders and not the idea" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 57:57; by: livingston; conf: medium)
- **Judge founders by what they could become, not what they are now.** This is why she had more faith in young founders than most investors did (said in both sources).
  - "we had to judge the founders not by what they were but what they could turn into" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 16:35; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "had more faith in young founders than most investors did back then" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 9:46; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "he was impressive at 19" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 55:40; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Start small and try things out before expanding.** Staying small keeps you nimble and open to change, and YC first ran batches to learn how to invest (said in both sources). See [[startups/fundamentals/start-small-and-stay-nimble]].
  - "start small so you can be nimble and open to change" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 25:10; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "to this day YC has a tradition of trying things out on a small scale before expanding them" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 25:10; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "the reason we did the batches was because we wanted to sort of learn how to be Angel Investors" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 16:22; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Ignore outside opinion unless it comes from your users.** The mainstream's view of your skills or idea does not matter, but users' views do. See [[founders/life/rejection-and-outside-opinion]].
  - "don't pay attention to the mainstream's opinion of what you're doing whether it's your skills your idea or whatever unless they're your users their opinion does not matter" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 23:59; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "although do pay attention to the opinion of your users they're important" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 23:59; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Pick a co-founder with complementary skills and the same moral compass.** Agree on the big questions, defer to each other on the small ones, and divide up the work. See [[hiring/cofounders/choosing-cofounders-and-early-hires]].
  - "find a co-founder with complementary skills but the same moral compass as you" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 23:59; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "we agreed on the big questions and we each deferred to each other's expertise on the small ones" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 24:36; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "Paul and I divided up responsibilities perfectly which I think is very important if you happen to start a start-up with your spouse or your partner" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 12:39; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Work on what genuinely interests you and play to your strengths.** A startup is so much work that you will quit otherwise. See [[founders/becoming-a-founder/do-you-need-the-typical-founder-profile]].
  - "do what you're genuinely genuinely interested in and try to play to your natural strengths a startup is so much work that you'll give up if you're not genuinely interested in it" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 23:59; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "the most successful startups grow organically out of the founders lives" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 20:36; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Help every founder you fund, not just your favorites.** Fund many, help them get started, and skip the board seats. See [[fundraising/investors/how-yc-was-designed-differently-from-vcs]].
  - "we had to help those Founders no matter what you couldn't pick your favorites and ignore the ones that were hopeless" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 11:16; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "make sure you don't get on the board of every company just fund a lot more help them get started have events" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 3:58; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Stay focused and don't act like a big company.** She avoids bureaucracy and anything that wastes founders' time.
  - "we were always trying to be focused and not be bureaucratic and not act like a big company" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 34:54; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "it is such a waste of time and so again that's just against Paul and I moral values" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 44:26; by: livingston; conf: high)

## How they reason
- **She generalizes from her own unlikely path.** Because she succeeded without the usual profile, she argues there is no single mold for a founder (said in both sources). See [[founders/becoming-a-founder/do-you-need-the-typical-founder-profile]].
  - "there's no one successful mold for a successful founder just because you might only see a certain type in the news that doesn't mean you need to turn yourself into that" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 23:24; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "don't edit your answers because as you can see from my example the most unlikely ingredients can often be the key to the recipe" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 22:49; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "it doesn't matter what your background is or where you went to school" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 47:48; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **She expects good new ideas to look silly at first.** Outlier ideas seem preposterous to everyone but the founders, so early ridicule is not a signal (said in both sources). See [[startups/ideas/when-everyone-says-your-idea-is-bad]].
  - "they're usually such outliers that they seemed preposterous at first to everyone except the founders" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 23:24; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "ideas always seem if they haven't been done yet they all seem sort of silly" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 12:56; by: livingston; conf: medium)
- **She treats YC itself as a startup.** It began as a hypothesis about what people wanted, filling a gap nothing else filled (said in both sources). See [[startups/fundamentals/start-small-and-stay-nimble]].
  - "Y Combinator began the same way as most other startups with a hypothesis about something we thought people wanted" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 0:33; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "we really did start it because there was nothing else like that at the time" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 1:43; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **She defines YC by contrast with VCs.** She asks what VCs don't do for founders and does that.
  - "VCS don't have weekly dinners with their Investments and we did" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 8:26; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "there needs to be a very early stage company that's just going to write a small check" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 2:16; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **She splits judgment by strength.** Technical people judge the idea; she reads people and the relationships between them. See [[founders/roles/role-of-the-non-technical-founder]].
  - "what YC needed was deeply technical people to judge the potential of an idea and then someone like me to understand the founders characters and the relationships between them" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 16:01; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "when it came to investing I had something that my co-founders didn't have I was a social radar" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 14:19; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **She looks at structure and incentives to explain behavior.** Having no LPs is what let YC take more risks and be kind to failing startups. See [[fundraising/investors/investors-and-failing-startups]].
  - "because YC didn't have any LPS at the time we weren't even constrained by a vague fiduciary responsibility to anyone and that allowed us to take more risks with who we chose to fund and it also allowed us to be benevolent to failing startups" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 19:26; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **She sees alumni as the network that compounds.** New founders are best helped by the ones who came before. See [[fundraising/investors/events-and-founder-community]].
  - "we have to get the alumni to help the new people" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 27:38; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "Our Best Contacts were our alumni" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 28:12; by: livingston; conf: high)

## Strong views
- **Against: investors squeezing founders over small outcomes.** She does not understand it, and says YC was driven by helping founders, not money. See [[fundraising/investors/investors-and-failing-startups]].
  - "to this day I still don't get why investors squeeze founders over small outcomes like this" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 20:36; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Against: conventional-minded founders.** You can't start a startup with a brand new idea if you think conventionally (said in both sources). See [[fundraising/investors/what-yc-looks-for-in-founders]].
  - "you cannot be conventional minded to start a startup with a brand new idea" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 51:09; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "I think one thing that we had in common and the founders had in common is that were unconventional you know independent-minded" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 19:17; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **Against: credentials as a gate.** Users don't care where you went to school. See [[founders/becoming-a-founder/do-you-need-the-typical-founder-profile]].
  - "it's okay not to have gone to an elite college" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 25:10; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "it's not true that every person can start every startup but a lot more people have what it takes to start some startup than realize it" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 22:17; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **For: boldness and pushing through rejection.** Working on ideas others call stupid means you will be ridiculed and rejected, so keep going (said in both sources). See [[founders/life/rejection-and-outside-opinion]].
  - "you do need a certain amount of boldness to work on ideas that most people would consider stupid and to keep going when you're ridiculed or ignored" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 25:45; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "you'll get rejected in so many different ways but you must just keep moving forward" ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]] @ 24:36; by: livingston; conf: high)
  - "all of a sudden the haters come out of the woodwork" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 33:49; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **For: free events for founders.** Students and founders have no money, so she made Startup School free. See [[fundraising/investors/events-and-founder-community]].
  - "you can't charge students to come they or startup Founders they don't have any money so we made it free" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 43:52; by: livingston; conf: high)
- **For: YC as a home for outsiders.** People who felt different or unconventional find their people there.
  - "it is a special place that all these people who might have felt like they were different or Outsiders or somehow unconventional come to YC and they think wow I found my people" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 48:21; by: livingston; conf: high)

- She is PG's wife and YC co-founder, so her praise of PG and of YC is not independent ([[yt-8d-cApFHjeY]]).
- Her 2024 account comes from a YC-produced podcast whose hosts were all funded by YC, so neither side is a neutral witness ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]]).

- She read several of PG's essays in draft, so her agreement with them is not fully independent ([[profile]]).

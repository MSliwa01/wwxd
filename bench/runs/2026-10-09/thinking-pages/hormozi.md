# How Alex Hormozi thinks

Built from all 13 compiled sources (2023-04 to 2026-08). Source counts are the number of distinct sources where the rule shows up. For detail, follow the leaf links.

## What they optimize for
- **Compounding over a long time, not shortcuts.** He says wealth comes from picking one path and sticking with it for years, and that staying in the game is what lets luck find you (4 sources; [[business/strategy/sticking-with-one-thing]], [[business/wealth-paths/four-paths-to-wealth]]).
  - "the richest people that I know pick one of these four paths, play it for a decade, and then end up with more money than everyone else that is just chasing shortcuts." ([[yt-sL16tsGafcQ]] @ 0:00; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "the effort that it takes to compound is the length of time you have to say no to everything else" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 22:58; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "You get lucky by staying in the game long enough for luck to find you." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 24:37; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "just comes down to doing 100 small things consistently over a very long period of time" ([[yt--j8_YCWZ05Q]] @ 19:11; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Leverage: more output for each unit of input.** He defines leverage as output minus input and wants to stack as many kinds as he can. More leverage also lets you set your own terms (2 sources; [[business/leverage/types-of-leverage]]).
  - "the people who move faster in life don't actually move faster they get more for every step" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:46:20; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "you want to Stack as many types of Leverage as you can and as much of them as you can" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:45:13; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "typically the more leverage you have, the further up this pyramid you can go because you can ultimately command your own terms." ([[yt-rMJIOK_FgJk]] @ 3:17; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Autonomy over other people's expectations.** He rejects "should" and says you'll be criticized whatever you do, so you may as well pick something worth the criticism (2 sources; [[mindset/others-opinions/caring-what-others-think]]).
  - "for me it's always been about autonomy" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:46:53; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "there is no should do what you want to do at least that's how I see the world" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:11:05; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "the idea is do things that it is worth being criticized for" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 2:17; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Trust and reputation over a bigger promise.** He would rather give up some of the promise to gain trust, and he turns down sales so his scarcity claims stay believable (2 sources; [[marketing/copywriting/damaging-admissions]], [[marketing/copywriting/urgency-and-scarcity]]).
  - "I would rather always sacrifice promise to gain trust" ([[yt-JDR-R--4HhM]] @ 13:09; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "it means you say no and you say no so you can maintain the reputation" ([[yt-JDR-R--4HhM]] @ 22:29; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "your reputation over time acts as almost an implied guarantee" ([[yt-uWdIgftpvBI]] @ 14:45; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Safety first, so you can afford risk later.** Cut costs, get out of the hole and bank cash before taking big swings, because you can't take risk with no time and no money (3 sources; [[mindset/personal-finance/first-100k-in-the-bank]], [[business/finance/paying-off-debt]]).
  - "The first step is that we have to cut all costs, so that we can take more risk." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 0:32; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "get yourself out of the hole as fast as humanly possible. Like do whatever you got to do to get your oxygen mask on." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 17:53; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "Risk when multiplied over a long time horizon tends to come due." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 9:10; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Speed.** He ranks a short wait for the result above a bigger result, and calls speed pure margin that people will pay extra for (3 sources; [[marketing/offers/speed-likelihood-and-ease]], [[pricing/speed-pricing/priority-fees]]).
  - "latency beats magnitude 7 days a week and twice on Sunday" ([[yt-uWdIgftpvBI]] @ 15:53; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "speed's all margin and in this business margin matters a lot." ([[yt-o64cI6tebnU]] @ 19:05; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "people will pay for speed, and layaway is a lever to force that on them." ([[yt-rMJIOK_FgJk]] @ 4:24; by: hormozi; conf: high)

## Rules they decide by
- **Do one thing, and subtract until it gets better.** Pick the one bet that would change everything and say no to the rest. His reasons: you can't go deep on many things at once, and overwhelm is what makes customers leave (said in 4 sources; [[business/strategy/focus-on-one-thing]], [[business/strategy/simplify-by-subtraction]], [[business/retention/overwhelm-and-onboarding]]).
  - "If we only did this one thing, would we accomplish all of our goals? And I think it's one of the most powerful questions." ([[yt--JLN1d1ZKUU]] @ 3:20; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "The price of wanting to be good at everything is being good at nothing." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 22:24; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "focus is achieved not through addition, but subtraction." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 4:18; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "The number one reason people churn, outside of price, is overwhelm. It is the churn driver. And this is why doing less can often retain more." ([[yt--j8_YCWZ05Q]] @ 9:18; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Do so much volume that failing would be unreasonable.** He counts repetitions, not talent or hours, and assumes the early attempts will fail (3 sources; [[mindset/skill-building/input-output-equations]]).
  - "if I do sales and I do more volume than everyone else on this team and I Shadow the guy who's the best and I do that for five years it's unreasonable that I won't be at least mediocre" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 47:15; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "you just have to do so much volume that it is unreasonable that you fail" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 9:04; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "it's not 10,000 hours, but 10,000 iterations." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 9:15; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Copy what the top 10% do, and out-rep the best person.** Find the best performers, see what they share, and make your next attempts look like theirs. Hire the best one-on-one if you can (2 sources; [[mindset/skill-building/learn-from-the-top-10-percent]], [[sales/learning-sales/high-volume-sales-practice]]).
  - "go Shadow the best guy and do twice the volume he's doing because... you're not as good as him" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 40:02; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "you just continue to do another 100 repetitions, look at the top 10, try and make the next 100 look like those 10" ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 10:52; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "The fastest way to learn skills is to find somebody who's really good and hire them one-on-one." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 9:48; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Learning only counts if your behavior changes.** Content you consume without acting on it is entertainment, not education (2 sources; [[mindset/skill-building/learning-requires-action]]).
  - "if you watch more videos, but your conditions are the same and your behavior does not change, you entertained yourself. You did not educate yourself." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 5:39; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "that is the definition of learning, which is same condition, new behavior." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 8:43; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Charge more, and sell to whoever values it most.** Start expensive and work your way down, because you need margin before you can serve the masses. A close rate of 80% or more means the price is too low; a price rise pays off as long as more than 65% still close (3 sources; [[pricing/premium-pricing/start-high-sell-your-time]], [[pricing/premium-pricing/raising-prices-to-close-more]], [[pricing/premium-pricing/sell-to-customers-who-value-it-most]]).
  - "either sell extremely expensive stuff to a select few or sell something super cheap to everyone. The middle is where people die." ([[yt-uWdIgftpvBI]] @ 0:00; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "find the people who value what you have the most" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:38:38; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "So whenever I hear 80% or over 80% close rates, I usually know that there's room in sale." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 8:06; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "As long as we close more than 65%, we make more money." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 8:06; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **If too few people say yes, fix the offer. If it already converts, leave it alone.** He calls the offer the highest-leverage thing in a business, and leads ads with it (3 sources; [[marketing/offers/value-equation]], [[marketing/advertising/testing-ad-creative]]).
  - "if we can't get enough people to say yes we need to make the offer better" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:25:23; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "The offer's already converting, so I don't really want to mess with that." ([[yt-MH-IMJxbUY4]] @ 24:06; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "Basically, just lead with the offer." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 15:38; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Spend as much as you can while the returns hold.** He keeps raising ad spend as long as CAC and ROAS hold and the business stays at least break-even (2 sources; [[marketing/advertising/scaling-spend-on-proven-returns]], [[business/business-models/money-models]]).
  - "the goal is to spend as much money as humanly possible uh while maintaining break even or better." ([[yt-tGhe3sBuk34]] @ 8:17; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "we just want to continue to spend month over month provided our ROAS and our CAC stay more or less the same." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 10:48; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Sell what people already pay for.** Don't bet on an untested idea. Research skills and products people already buy, and mine buyer reviews for their words (2 sources; [[business/entrepreneurship/choosing-what-to-sell]]).
  - "the best way to look is not to risk it on something that you don't know, but find stuff people are already spending money on." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 14:09; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "go into the books that people are buying in your niche and then extract the reviews and then get the quotes that are specific to their pain" ([[yt-uWdIgftpvBI]] @ 13:04; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Retention thresholds.** If fewer than 7 in 10 customers stay each month, fix retention before anything else. Churn falls sharply after day 90 and again by month six (1 source, Skool data; [[business/retention/measuring-churn]]).
  - "if less than seven out of 10 people want to stay on a monthly basis, you probably need to work on your retention more than anything else. It'll be the highest ROI lever." ([[yt--j8_YCWZ05Q]] @ 1:39; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "if you keep someone 90 days, which seems to be the first kind of big churn drop-off point, it drops to around 10% or less" ([[yt--j8_YCWZ05Q]] @ 3:53; by: hormozi; conf: high)

## How they reason
- **Defines the terms, then cuts the problem down to two levers.** "Grow" becomes "get more customers or make each one worth more", which he calls the core arbitrage of every business (2 sources; [[business/growth/finding-the-constraint]]).
  - "I just make a make an attempt to define the terms that a lot of us use every day and then it makes a... lot easier to solve for those things in business" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 35:05; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "a lot of people like I want to grow my business I'm like all right what does that mean like well get more customers make them worth more okay so it's one of those two things" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 35:37; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "fundamentally all businesses have the cost of getting customers and what you make from those customers as the core economic arbitrage that makes a business a business" ([[yt-uWdIgftpvBI]] @ 0:00; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Finds the constraint first.** In every consult he opens by asking whether the business is limited by supply or by demand, and then works only on that (3 sources; [[business/growth/finding-the-constraint]]).
  - "we have to identify what the constraint of the of the system is" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:30:19; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "is this a supply- or demand-constrained business?" ([[yt-MH-IMJxbUY4]] @ 4:56; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "the first problem I try to delineate is this is a supply constraint business or a demand constraint business." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 3:47; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Fixes what already exists, cheapest first, before trying anything new.** In consults he improves the current channel, landing page and creative before he'll add a new channel (2 sources; [[business/growth/incremental-improvement]]).
  - "I feel like there's a lot more that you can work on the existing one by like a lot." ([[yt-MH-IMJxbUY4]] @ 5:27; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "New channel, little too hard for right now in my opinion." ([[yt-MH-IMJxbUY4]] @ 24:06; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "Like no Hail Mary's. This is just consistent yardage." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 4:19; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Treats businesses as following patterns.** That's why he applies the same playbook across industries. He adds that the steps are the same near zero and get more tailored as a business grows (2 sources; [[business/growth/patterns-across-business-sizes]]).
  - "That's because businesses behave in patterns." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 5:54; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "if you're at zero, the steps are identical. And if you're at a zillion, the steps are entirely unique." ([[yt-o64cI6tebnU]] @ 14:45; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Thinks in inputs and outputs, and extrapolates from his own track record.** His confidence comes from evidence that he repeats what he has done before, not from charisma (2 sources; [[mindset/skill-building/input-output-equations]], [[business/entrepreneurship/what-makes-a-good-entrepreneur]]).
  - "if you do not know the inputs that are going to get the output that you want then what are you doing" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 38:58; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "I try and make statements that I believe are unreasonable that if I do it enough it will be true and so that's for me that's what gives me the confidence" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 49:26; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "extrapolate your past success of any kind to your next success" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 10:12; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Frames choices as trades and risk that can be priced.** He asks what trade you're unwilling to make and who carries the risk. He says people misprice risk because they count failures instead of the absolute return (2 sources; [[mindset/emotions/sadness-and-perceived-options]], [[business/deal-structure/risk-and-compensation]]).
  - "the question to ask is, What trade am I knowingly or unknowingly unwilling to make?" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 19:01; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "The thing that is always the the overarching umbrella... is who's got the risk. And the more you shift that risk in your favor, the more you get paid." ([[yt-rMJIOK_FgJk]] @ 9:22; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "the part that people misprice risk is that they count in number of failures rather than the absolute return." ([[yt-rMJIOK_FgJk]] @ 15:51; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Makes things specific.** He uses the same move for his own fears and for persuading customers: in his words, both fear and pain only exist in the specific (3 sources; [[marketing/copywriting/specificity-and-moments]]).
  - "fear only exists in the vague not in the specific" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 20:46; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "pain and persuasion only exist in the specific, never the vague" ([[yt-uWdIgftpvBI]] @ 13:40; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "what creates compelling copy specificity" ([[yt-JDR-R--4HhM]] @ 8:05; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Lets the market and tests decide, not his own taste.** He ships many variations, counts silence as feedback too, and keeps the winners (4 sources; [[marketing/advertising/testing-ad-creative]], [[marketing/advertising/ugc-content-loop]]).
  - "as long as you can humble yourself and listen to the market, it will tell you where you suck" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 16:13; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "You post this content, nothing happens. That is still feedback." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 9:48; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "every week we just blast off 30 videos up there and we see where the winners are." ([[yt-MH-IMJxbUY4]] @ 8:43; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "The cheapest ways of testing new ads um is just like post them as organic and then see how they do." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 14:02; by: hormozi; conf: high)

## Strong views
- **Against: complexity.** He calls it lazy thinking that adds friction. In copy it costs sales, and in a product it gives customers more reasons to cancel (3 sources; [[business/strategy/simplify-by-subtraction]], [[marketing/copywriting/simple-copy]]).
  - "complexity is typically the child of lazy thinking" ([[yt--JLN1d1ZKUU]] @ 1:43; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "fancy equals friction simple equals sales" ([[yt-JDR-R--4HhM]] @ 32:07; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "all we did is we gave them more reason to cancel because they never used it" ([[yt--j8_YCWZ05Q]] @ 10:54; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Against: shortcuts and chasing the next shiny thing.** He says wanting a fast way to get rich keeps people poor, and that switching every six months kills compounding (2 sources; [[business/wealth-paths/four-paths-to-wealth]]).
  - "Poor people stay poor because they want a fast way to get rich." ([[yt-sL16tsGafcQ]] @ 0:00; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "People underestimate how much effort it takes to stick with one thing rather than jumping to the next shiny opportunity every six months." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 22:24; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Against: living by other people's opinions.** He reminds himself that people stop caring once you're dead, and that you'll be criticized whatever you do (2 sources; [[mindset/others-opinions/caring-what-others-think]], [[mindset/career/quitting-your-job]]).
  - "if there's one thing that I I will try and beat out of me until I die is caring what other people think" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:14:28; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "If you do nothing or you do anything, people will criticize you." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 2:17; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **For: proof and admitting flaws.** His reasons: competitors can copy an offer but not proof, and admitting flaws builds trust (2 sources; [[marketing/copywriting/proof-and-authority]], [[marketing/copywriting/damaging-admissions]]).
  - "proof will always outdo promise" ([[yt-JDR-R--4HhM]] @ 3:44; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "if you want to stand out in a crowded marketplace tell the truth" ([[yt-JDR-R--4HhM]] @ 15:39; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "on the sales call, I want to basically front-load all of our damaging admissions." ([[yt-MH-IMJxbUY4]] @ 19:09; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **For: taking more risk than most people do, when it's mispriced.** He says pay tracks risk, not brains or effort, and that most people take too little of it (3 sources; [[business/deal-structure/risk-and-compensation]]).
  - "really good entrepreneurs is that they understand risk better than most people, which typically means that most people don't take enough of it." ([[yt-rMJIOK_FgJk]] @ 15:51; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "you get rewarded for the risk you take on. You cannot take any risk on if you have no time and no money." ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]] @ 13:04; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "this is just part of leveling up in business is that you take greater and greater risk, and at the same time, you get greater and greater rewards." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 11:54; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **For: concentration over diversification.** He says spreading your bets also lowers your upside, and that you get one real bet, so make it count (2 sources; [[business/wealth-paths/investing-as-a-wealth-path]], [[business/strategy/focus-on-one-thing]]).
  - "whenever you uh distribute your bets, you also decrease your upside" ([[yt-sL16tsGafcQ]] @ 13:06; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "you have one real bet that you can make on it. And so it's like you want to make that bet worth it." ([[yt--JLN1d1ZKUU]] @ 10:27; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **For: pain and negative motivation as fuel.** He says pain motivates faster than pleasure, and that suffering is fixed, so pick a goal worth suffering for (2 sources; [[mindset/motivation/pain-as-motivation]], [[mindset/suffering/goals-worth-suffering-for]]).
  - "pain motivates significantly faster and stronger than pleasure does" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 51:43; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "Suffering is a fixed cost of life. So pick a life, pick a goal worth suffering for." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 0:34; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **For: working very hard while you have few responsibilities.** He works all the time himself and says a few hard years early on compound for decades (2 sources; [[mindset/work/working-hard]]).
  - "I work all the time I have no Hobbies besides working out" ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]] @ 1:09:28; by: hormozi; conf: high)
  - "the compounding effects of a few years of hard work like this can set you up for your life for decades" ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 11:52; by: hormozi; conf: high)

## Where they've changed their mind
- **How hard other people should work (2023-04 → 2025-11).** He went from "there is no should" to prescribing maximum hours for people under 30 with no responsibilities. This reads more as a shift in emphasis than a reversal. See [[tensions]].
- **Price complaints (early career → 2026-03).** He used to ignore every "it costs too much". Now he tags cancellation reasons and adds a lower tier when ideal customers raise price, but still ignores complaints from people outside his target customer. See [[tensions]], [[business/retention/price-objections-and-annual-plans]].
- **Risk vs. debt (2026-03 → 2026-06).** He is pro-risk in how you get paid, yet he says "I tend to skew like very close to the Ramsey side" ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 8:37; by: hormozi; conf: high) on business debt. A month later he describes heavy debt as the way fund managers get rich. He doesn't reconcile the two. See [[tensions]].
- **Big bets vs. small steps (2025-05 vs. 2026-05).** Not a reversal, but the scope matters. When choosing a company's one priority he says "We're not thinking increments. We're thinking orders of magnitude." ([[yt--JLN1d1ZKUU]] @ 9:55; by: hormozi; conf: high). When fixing a small business that already works he says "Like no Hail Mary's. This is just consistent yardage." ([[yt-OQf2Ba-Lp_4]] @ 4:19; by: hormozi; conf: high).

## Blind spots
- **Self-interest.** His free content funnels businesses to Acquisition.com, his books and Skool, and his retention advice is built on data from Skool, a platform he promotes ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]], [[yt--j8_YCWZ05Q]], [[yt-sL16tsGafcQ]]). Discount his recommendations of those products. See [[profile]].
- **Survivorship.** His case for taking the leap rests on his own near-ruin turnaround. He concedes that "the vast majority of entrepreneurs make less. But there are a select few entrepreneurs who make significantly more." ([[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]] @ 25:46; by: hormozi; conf: high)
- **Fund management.** His GP-economics example is a whiteboard illustration with round numbers, not his own fund's results ([[yt-sL16tsGafcQ]]).
- **Frugality and hours rules that assume no dependents.** His "No exceptions" cost-cutting and his maximum-hours advice are aimed at young people without family obligations ([[yt-jfW6gL6hKhk]], [[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]]).
- **Psychology, happiness and history.** His claims about the hedonic baseline, sadness as a perceived lack of options, and similar are personal frames, not expertise ([[yt-x3e73Qn6NOo]], [[yt-XGm2ERU9qtA]]).
- **Borrowed frameworks.** Several of his ideas restate Jobs, Slootman, Bezos, Marks and others ([[yt--JLN1d1ZKUU]], [[yt-rMJIOK_FgJk]]).
- **Self-reported numbers.** He gives different revenue figures for the same book launch across sources, so treat his self-reported figures with care. See [[tensions]].

# How Michael Seibel thinks

## What they optimize for
- **Learning from real users as early as possible.** He says research and planning tell you little until people can use something, and many founders quit before any user touches their product. See [[product/users/getting-first-users-and-feedback]] and [[product/mvp/launch-quickly]].
  - "all the research in the world is good, but until you can put something in front of people, you have no faking idea whether it's going to work." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 10:39; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "You'd be surprised at how many founders journeys end before a single user has actually interacted with a product they've created." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 2:14; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "It's a lot harder to learn from your customers when they don't have a product they can play with." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 10:39; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **The customer's problem, not the founder's solution.** He holds the problem and the customer fixed and treats the product as something to change until it works. See [[product/mvp/iterate-dont-fall-in-love-with-your-mvp]].
  - "hold the problem you're solving tightly, hold the customer tightly, hold the solution you're building loosely." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 3:19; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "if your screwdriver doesn't help the mechanic solve the problem, keep the mechanic. Keep the problem." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 3:53; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "I think for better or for worse the consistent problem has always been how do you help your customers be more successful?" ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 6:59; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Knowing the problem firsthand.** He favors founders who are their own users, because they can tell whether the product works and are not steered by every request. (said in 3 sources) See [[startups/ideas/solve-your-own-problem]] and [[product/users/acting-on-user-feature-requests]].
  - "It's even more helpful if you're your own user so you can tell whether your product's working for you." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 0:33; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "i think the last thing is they used their own product" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 4:24; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "knowing yourself about the problem is way more important now. Because like, if you don't know about the problem and users are telling you features and they're excited, and you don't know what the hell's going on, you can get yourself in a like you you you basically have created a tar pit." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 3:47; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Founder time spent on the company, not on wasted motion.** He would rather founders build something customers or investors want than polish pitches or drag out a dead situation. (said in 3 sources) See [[startups/strategy/building-leverage-before-negotiating]] and [[hiring/cofounders/handling-a-cofounder-breakup]].
  - "spending all that time on a pitch deck is not as valuable as spending your time building anything that you can give to a customer." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 11:11; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "if you can invest that time in making your company something that investors will chase after versus decksmithing, you're you're playing into the odds versus playing out of the odds." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 10:03; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "when you really know the probability of success is zero, most important thing is to stop wasting time." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 9:26; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **A mission big enough that people care.** He says the road is hard either way, and a big goal is what lets you build a team and a movement. See [[startups/ideas/how-ambitious-should-your-idea-be]].
  - "This road's going to be hard regardless so you might as well make the world a better place." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 19:37; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "no one's going to tell you in the back of your mind like, yeah, after that no one's going to care. And you need people to care." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 10:56; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "Their skill was understanding having high ambition allowed them to build the most exciting largest team and that team would power them to fulfilling the ambition." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 10:24; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **The company's future over past contributions.** When a co-founder leaves, he wants equity kept for the people who stay, since most of the value is still to be created. See [[hiring/cofounders/splitting-equity-when-a-cofounder-leaves]].
  - "the vast majority of the value this company could create hasn't been created yet." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 14:47; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "you need to make sure that that company has as much equity as possible to motivate the people who are there" ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 14:47; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "Like, you get to hedge. The founder staying doesn't." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 15:21; by: seibel; conf: medium)

## Rules they decide by
- **Launch something bad quickly, and count the launch as the day you get any customer.** See [[product/mvp/launch-quickly]].
  - "if you can walk away from one thing from this presentation, it's launch something bad quickly." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 1:41; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "How about launch is when you get any customers." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 10:07; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "Once you get anything out in the world, the momentum to keep anything going is extremely strong." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 12:17; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **Build the MVP for a small set of users and their biggest problems, and ignore the rest for now.** See [[product/mvp/how-to-plan-an-mvp]].
  - "they should just focus on a small set of initial users and their highest order problems and then ignore the rest until later." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 5:03; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "You should have a vision of everyone. You should have an MVP very small. All this is is a base to iterate from." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 5:03; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "we see startups just start with a landing page and a spreadsheet." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 4:29; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **Write the spec down, time box it, and cut features until it fits.** He says an unwritten plan drifts from weeks to months.
  - "time box your spec. So your spec is a list of stuff you need to build before you launch." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 11:11; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "because you never wrote it down, you don't even really realize you're changing it. And so, your three-week plan turns into a three-month plan." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 11:44; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "Just cut the stuff that clearly isn't important. Um, and if there's no non-important things, start cutting important things." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 12:17; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **Find your first user by talking to someone you know has the problem.** He is wary of products built for users the founder can't name. See [[product/users/getting-first-users-and-feedback]].
  - "So the way you get your first user is you talk to that person that you know has the problem." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 1:07; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "if you are building a product for a mysterious set of users that you have no idea who they are, question that slightly." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 1:07; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **If your smart technical friends all complain about the same thing, treat it as a real problem.** See [[startups/ideas/solve-your-own-problem]].
  - "if all of your smart technical friends are complaining about the same thing maybe it's a real problem" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 14:14; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "it was a problem that they saw their peers in yc have" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 14:14; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **In a co-founder breakup where the stock is worth nothing, keep it cheap, fast and clean.** He sets small equity numbers for the leaver and skips expensive lawyers. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-a-cofounder-breakup]].
  - "in a situation where you're having a co-founder breakup and the stock is worth nothing, paying lawyers a bunch of money makes no sense." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 11:36; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "After a number of years, 5% typical. Under a year, 1%, 2%, sometimes less than 1%." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 21:22; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "Do reasonable severance, sign clean paperwork and and peace out" ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 18:39; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **When someone leaves, act as the responsible party and be generous.** He says the rest of the company is watching, and kindness costs little.
  - "understand that everyone in your company is looking at you right now." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 17:33; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "it is so cheap to be nice." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 17:33; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "if you think of yourself as the responsible party and you act that way, often you don't trigger victimness on the other side, too." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 18:06; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **In sales, keep filling the funnel instead of waiting on one deal.** He says customers don't want to feel a company needs them more than they need it. See [[growth/tactics/getting-leverage-in-sales]].
  - "when they're with that deal that they really want to close, they have such a hard time doing more outreach, getting more meetings, putting more deals into the funnel." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 12:51; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "if you're a customer, you don't like to feel that the company you're considering needs you more than you need them." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 13:56; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **In hiring, compete with other early startups, not Google, and sell the responsibility and adventure.** See [[hiring/recruiting/winning-at-hiring]].
  - "you're probably competing against that other early stage startup, not Google" ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 16:40; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "it turns out you have more leverage than you think when you're looking to hire people who are excited for the adventure." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 17:13; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "if someone was good, we had a printed and signed offer letter like like that was handed to them at the end of their last interview" ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 18:51; by: seibel; conf: high)

## How they reason
- **He reasons from the crude first versions of companies that later won.** He points out how bad they were at the start and how far they moved from the first idea. See [[product/mvp/how-to-plan-an-mvp]].
  - "all of these are billion-dollar companies and they all started with something that most people would say is pretty shitty." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 7:52; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "one of the things that you might be interested in about in Airbnb's first product is that there were no payments." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 5:37; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "none of the products I showed you before was the initial vision, what it ended up being." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 12:51; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **He asks why the existing products are bad, not who was first.** He looks for room to be 10x better than an incumbent. See [[startups/ideas/competing-with-existing-products]].
  - "there was a pre-existing product pre-existing competitor dominant you might say but there was an opportunity to make something that was 10x better than what existed" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 0:33; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "i think it's interesting to talk about why the existing products weren't great because honestly that's important" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 2:10; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "so once again not first mover" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 13:40; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **He draws on his own founder mistakes and near misses.** He names what he got wrong at Justin.tv and what the crisis taught him. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-cofounder-conflict]].
  - "another thing that was a misconception that I had was that each co-founder is going to add an equal amount of value at all times." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 6:36; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "at any given time, I think we had two and a half co-founders." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 7:42; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "we got to figure out how to make this thing profitable or we're going to die." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 6:44; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **He checks whose business model a choice feeds.** Lawyers, press and service providers can profit from a founder's anger or hope. See [[growth/tactics/press-vs-telling-your-own-story]].
  - "a lawyer can suck up infinite amount of your ... anger and monetize it." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 12:07; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "Any time you're spending money, be worried that someone's taking advantage." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 19:13; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "this poor founder is basically like feeding the the business model of this blog." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 21:02; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **He separates the lasting problem from the changing constraint.** When tools change, he keeps the goal and redoes the reasoning. See [[product/mvp/mvp-scope-when-features-are-cheap]].
  - "it's always been a theory of old to build every feature a user wants, but now it's an exponential theory of old ... because you can." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 3:14; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "we have to disagree with our former advice or there's a different set of constraints now. It used to be the case you had to figure out what's the smallest set of features to build that would create value because building features was costly." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 0:33; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **He thinks about how a company's story spreads through other people.** A pitch has to stick in the minds of those who retell it. See [[startups/ideas/how-ambitious-should-your-idea-be]].
  - "You don't know how many times other people pitch your company." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 7:37; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **He treats a bold goal as more reachable than it looks.** He says the knowledge to start is often found by curiosity, not insider access. See [[startups/ideas/making-an-ambitious-idea-credible]].
  - "I want people to understand that that might not be as hard as you think." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 14:43; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "Figuring out who the leading AI researchers was ... a question of curiosity. It wasn't a state secret." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 14:43; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "you don't have to have all the valuable insights before you start" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 11:31; by: seibel; conf: medium)

## Strong views
- **Against: low-ambition, venture-funded startups.** He says small venture-funded businesses rarely pay off, and tiny optimizations can't rally a team. See [[startups/ideas/how-ambitious-should-your-idea-be]].
  - "there aren't a lot of venture funded small businesses that make anyone any money." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 1:40; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "Are we changing the world or are we optimizing click-throughs by 1%?" ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 8:10; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "so hard to build a movement off of improving click-through rates by 1% by using AI." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 10:56; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Against: taking the consensus that your idea is bad as a verdict.** He says Airbnb and Coinbase would not exist if their founders had listened. See [[startups/ideas/when-everyone-says-your-idea-is-bad]].
  - "most people founders and investors thought these ideas were horrible either because they were too hard to execute or because they just were bad ideas" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 0:33; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "if they had listened to most of the investors or most of the founders at the time airbnb wouldn't exist" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 7:08; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "if he had taken the demo day signal the investor signal of oh we don't want to give you money and quit we don't have coinbase" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 12:03; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Against: scenesters.** He means people who play at startups out of fear of how experts see them, without trying to do anything real. See [[startups/ideas/making-an-ambitious-idea-credible]].
  - "wants to play the game of startup but not actually do anything" ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 16:51; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "I think a scenester would be one very afraid of being looked down upon by experts." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 16:51; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "do not be fooled by the scene-sters, by the fake people out there." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 20:09; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Against: the big press launch.** He says launches aren't special, so press can wait while getting customers can't. See [[product/mvp/launch-quickly]].
  - "So, it turns out that launches aren't that special at all." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 10:07; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "Let's push the press launch off and let's push the get any customers launch really really soon." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 10:39; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **Against: falling in love with your MVP.** He says founders who love what they built go looking for new problems for it, when the first version matters about as much as a first-grade paper. See [[product/mvp/iterate-dont-fall-in-love-with-your-mvp]].
  - "a lot of founders once they've figured out how to build something fall in love with it. And so if it doesn't work for a certain set of users, they start thinking, well, I wonder what other problems this thing can solve." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 3:19; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "you wouldn't fall in love with a paper you wrote in the first grade and like that's like the level of impact often your MVP has." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 12:51; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **For: a high price that people still pay, as proof of an insight.** See [[product/pricing/charging-from-the-start]].
  - "that's how you know you have an insight like when you're the most expensive thing" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 16:25; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **Against: expecting co-founders to contribute equally at all times.** He says the expectation breeds resentment, and friends often don't say it or say it softer. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-cofounder-conflict]].
  - "I see a lot of resentment build up when somebody feels like oh, I'm grinding and my co-founder's not." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 6:36; by: seibel; conf: medium)
  - "because you're friends with this person, more often than not, you're not going to say it, or you're going to put it slightly differently" ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 3:49; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Against: blaming the departing co-founder.** He says founders' own list of mistakes is longer than their grievances. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-a-cofounder-breakup]].
  - "If you made a list of all the things we screwed up in our company, it would be way longer than the things you're pissed at your co-founder about." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 21:54; by: seibel; conf: high)

## Where they've changed their mind
- **MVP scope (2019 to 2026).** In 2019 he said to build a lean MVP in weeks so you can learn from users. In 2026 he said "we have to disagree with our former advice". The conclusion stays the same (keep the MVP small), but the reason changes: AI makes features nearly free, so the limit is the founder's discipline to edit. See [[tensions]], [[product/mvp/how-to-plan-an-mvp]] and [[product/mvp/mvp-scope-when-features-are-cheap]].
  - "In most cases, most people should be building a very lean MVP. So, by that we mean um you should be able to build it fast in weeks, not months." ([[yt-1hHMwLxN6EM]] @ 4:29; by: seibel; conf: high)
  - "we have to disagree with our former advice or there's a different set of constraints now. It used to be the case you had to figure out what's the smallest set of features to build that would create value because building features was costly." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 0:33; by: seibel; conf: medium)
- **Disagreement, not a change: pricing vs. PG.** Seibel (2022) treats a high price people still pay as proof of an insight. PG (2026) advises selling cheaply at first. See [[tensions]].
  - "that's how you know you have an insight like when you're the most expensive thing" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 16:25; by: seibel; conf: high)
- **Disagreement, not a change: AI and startup winners vs. Tan and Altman.** In 2026 Seibel said employees with a clear job may gain more from AI tools than startups do. Altman and Tan expect bigger startup winners. See [[tensions]] and [[startups/ai/will-ai-coding-create-more-winners]].
  - "an employee inside of a company is now so much more empowered with these tools when they have a clear job to do and they know how to do it. And now it's like they don't have to tax the company's resources, they probably get a much higher multiplier effect than the startup." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 9:10; by: seibel; conf: medium)

## Blind spots
- Several of his 2026 episodes are sponsored by Standard Capital, the Series A fund Caldwell co-founded and partners in. That covers the MVP, co-founder and ambition episodes ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]]; [[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]]; [[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]]). The 2026-10 fundraising episode is co-hosted by Caldwell, an active investor whose fund sponsors the show ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]]). See [[profile]].
- His case against low-ambition startups is an investor's lens. Venture returns depend on rare huge outcomes, and he limits the case to venture-funded companies ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]]).
- His "ignore the skeptics" lesson comes from three YC winners picked in hindsight (Airbnb, Coinbase, Stripe). It also defends YC against its old reputation for funding knock-offs, so survivorship and institutional interest both shape it ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]]). His ambition examples (Musk, OpenAI, Obama's campaign) are also winners in hindsight ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]]).
- His advice to skip the press and "tell your own story" comes from someone who runs his own show ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]]).

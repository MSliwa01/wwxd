# How Dalton Caldwell thinks

## What they optimize for
- **A startup that works beats a good pitch.** He would rather a founder be bad at fundraising with something working than good at it with nothing working. See [[fundraising/investors/how-to-convince-investors]] and [[fundraising/investors/why-investors-string-founders-along]].
  - "it's way better to be like a very bad fundraiser ... with something that's working than someone that's a very good fundraiser with ... something that's not working at all." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 5:35; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Leverage, meaning no single deal matters too much.** He defines leverage by how much you care if one deal dies, and warns against betting everything on one customer. See [[startups/strategy/building-leverage-before-negotiating]] and [[growth/tactics/getting-leverage-in-sales]].
  - "it's almost like the definition of leverage in this situation is that if any deal dies how much do you care" ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 13:23; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "if you get no signups and there's really only one possible customer you can get, you put all of your eggs in the basket of that one customer." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 11:43; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Solving the user's short-term problem, not the founder's big vision.** Users signed up for his product only after he cut most of it. See [[product/mvp/mvp-scope-when-features-are-cheap]].
  - "I had this expansive vision of what it could be, but man, like users don't care about that. They just want to know how to solve their short-term problems." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 6:59; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "when we stripped out 80% of the functionality ... they were like, ... I'm I'm signing up right now." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 6:27; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **Not wasting years on an idea that can't get big.** He calls a low-ambition idea you know can never be big a tarpit. See [[founders/becoming-a-founder/what-if-you-lack-ambition]].
  - "If you burn years of your life on something that you think is a waste of time in your heart of hearts that is a true tarpit." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 20:40; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "But it's not obvious spending your time working on a low ambition startup idea for years is like actually a good idea." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 17:57; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **A long-term reputation.** He treats a founder's career as an infinite game, where being known as scorched earth ends it. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-a-cofounder-breakup]].
  - "I would encourage people to think about this as like a game theory infinite games situation" ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 15:54; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "being known as the super litigious scorched earth person, you're never going to work again." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 16:28; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Keeping the founder's hope alive.** He says most startups die because the founder gives up, and a co-founder is someone to talk to. See [[hiring/cofounders/do-you-need-a-cofounder]].
  - "the number one cause of death when you're a founder is it just sort of dies on the vine and you run out of hope and you give up on it." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 2:11; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "there is no way you can get around not having someone to talk to all the time to bounce ideas off of." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 3:14; by: caldwell; conf: medium)

## Rules they decide by
- **Add a feature when users pull for it, not because AI can build it.** Cheap building means the founder has to edit harder. See [[product/mvp/mvp-scope-when-features-are-cheap]] and [[product/users/acting-on-user-feature-requests]].
  - "I think the advice here founders is you have to try harder than ever to resist the temptation of building super complex broad surface area products. And of course you should add more features to it, but it should be because users are pulling you towards building more stuff, I think, and not because Codex could crank it out." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 4:19; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "I think that you also have to be more of an editor than you did in the past." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 2:09; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **In a co-founder breakup, skip who was right and plan the separation.** Start from no fault on both sides. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-a-cofounder-breakup]].
  - "the facts don't matter. I'm not the judge and jury." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 9:57; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "Let's just try to create a go-forward plan that is the most positive for all parties involved to not work together anymore." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 10:29; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "start with the way it should go is sort of no fault from both sides." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 13:42; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Don't say or do anything in a split you can't undo.** Don't try to ruin the other person.
  - "Don't try to ruin the other person. Don't try to ruin the reputation." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 13:42; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "Make it so that you don't say things you can't take back." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 14:14; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Treat hiring like sales and build a big pipeline.** Most broken hiring he sees has no pipeline at all. See [[hiring/recruiting/winning-at-hiring]].
  - "when I'm debugging um founders hiring pipeline, the biggest issue is they don't have a pipeline." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 15:01; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "you probably should be putting as much effort into that as you are into sales." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 17:45; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "you're basically selling a job at your company." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 18:17; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Pair a big ambition with a simple pitch of small, credible steps.** The leaps should feel intuitive, and you should be the person who can make them. See [[startups/ideas/making-an-ambitious-idea-credible]].
  - "The ambition has to be combined with a simple pitch" ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 13:08; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "a pitch where the logical leaps you are making are intuitive and that you are credible at making those logical leaps." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 13:08; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "make very simple low leap of faith arguments on how you'll be able to do it." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 14:43; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **If you can't make your idea ambitious, go work somewhere that is.** He thinks that may be more fulfilling than years on a small idea. See [[founders/becoming-a-founder/what-if-you-lack-ambition]].
  - "to the extent that you have no idea how to make your startup ambitious, it might actually be more fun and fulfilling to go work somewhere where that is not the" ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 17:24; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Get to know a co-founder before you go through something intense together.** He says the root cause of fights is starting the relationship for the wrong reasons. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-cofounder-conflict]].
  - "The biggest root cause is that you end up getting into the relationship for the wrong reasons." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 4:54; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "if you end up getting thrown into a pretty intense experience with a complete stranger, and you've taken no time to understand the other person, the chances that you'll have a disagreement are pretty high." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 4:54; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Use vesting so a split is clean.** He says it protects both sides. See [[hiring/cofounders/splitting-equity-when-a-cofounder-leaves]].
  - "Vesting protects both parties in this case because it makes it cleaner to separate." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 14:14; by: caldwell; conf: medium)

## How they reason
- **He tests a product on real users and cuts what confuses them.** He ran user calls before launching his own product and simplified it from what he heard. See [[product/users/acting-on-user-feature-requests]].
  - Ran 12 Zoom calls with founders he knew who could be users; many didn't understand the product because it was too complicated ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 5:54; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - Stripped out about 80% of the functionality before launch, after which a founder signed up during the call ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 6:27; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **He judges a market by how bad the existing product felt to him as a customer.** He had used the incumbents before Airbnb and Stripe and remembers the pain. See [[startups/ideas/competing-with-existing-products]].
  - "to get a merchant credit card account um was like applying for a mortgage" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 12:34; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "it kind of felt like the third-rate entrant to a crowded space at the time" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 1:38; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **He thinks one real insight is enough to build a huge company on.** Big market stories are not needed. See [[startups/ideas/estimating-market-size]].
  - "that was enough of an insight to be the basis for like a hundred billion dollar company he didn't need to do all this other stuff" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 10:56; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "even brian in his yc application didn't really believe in his own idea" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 10:23; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **He doesn't judge or copy how other founders made it work.** Each team's setup is its own. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-cofounder-conflict]].
  - "if you try to stand in judgement of how other people made it work, ... you're wasting your time." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 8:17; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "don't assume you could just blindly copy or cargo cult another person's playbook and it will necessarily work for you." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 8:51; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **He weighs a risky choice by its odds, not by whether it can fail.** Many good ideas carry risk. See [[hiring/cofounders/do-you-need-a-cofounder]].
  - "there are many things in life where I think they're a good idea, but they involve taking a risk and they don't have a 100% success rate." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 1:38; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "part of the reason there's so much I never want to have a co-founder because I heard my friend and this happened to them and blah blah blah blah. It's cuz it was handled horribly." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 20:18; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **He separates more builders from more winners.** Cheaper tools let more people be found, but that does not mean more companies win. See [[startups/ai/will-ai-coding-create-more-winners]].
  - "my theory is the democratization of these tools is awesome and it's going to allow more young people to be discovered" ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 8:37; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "number of winners is not obviously going to go up." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 9:10; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **He checks whether a pitcher can back up a bold claim.** His model ambitious founders said provocative things and could explain them. See [[startups/ideas/how-ambitious-should-your-idea-be]] and [[startups/ideas/making-an-ambitious-idea-credible]].
  - "he would say crazy provocative things, he would back them up" ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 9:18; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "When we heard Sam pitch OpenAI, he actually knew the researchers that were doing the work and were recruiting them to work on it." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 14:11; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "So you would need the combination of a credible argument that you could explain and sit down for half an hour and like walk through all the pieces." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 16:20; by: caldwell; conf: medium)

## Strong views
- **For: building what you yourself would want.** He credits Stripe with asking what a programmer would want and making developers love it. See [[growth/tactics/building-for-developers]] and [[startups/ideas/solve-your-own-problem]].
  - "what would i want or what would it what would a programmer want" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 15:18; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "they went nuts at making something that developers would just freak out about and everyone copied it" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 15:18; by: caldwell; conf: high)
  - "it was like if steve jobs had to design a payments company it would be stripe" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 15:52; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **Against: chasing press.** He says getting press helps a startup surprisingly little. See [[growth/tactics/press-vs-telling-your-own-story]].
  - "too many founders were obsessed of getting the press to write about them" ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 19:54; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "it is shocking how little um getting press helps a startup." ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]] @ 20:28; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **For: ideas that experts have written off, if you can explain them.** He wants a credible argument for an idea experts say will never work. See [[startups/ideas/when-everyone-says-your-idea-is-bad]].
  - "And have it be an idea that has been discarded by experts that it will never work." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 16:20; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "people saw bitcoin as a tiny market that it was going to crash that it was a bubble" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 9:50; by: caldwell; conf: high)
- **Against: avoiding a crowded market just because it is crowded.** Stripe entered a market of huge incumbents. See [[startups/ideas/competing-with-existing-products]].
  - "it was seen as a crowded market with huge companies with hundreds of employees thousands of employees" ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]] @ 17:31; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **For: having a co-founder, or something like one.** He says single founders who succeeded built co-founder-like relationships. See [[hiring/cofounders/do-you-need-a-cofounder]].
  - "the issue of not having a co-founder is you don't have anyone to talk about and you don't feel like you're on a mission, you don't feel like you're on an adventure." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 2:11; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
  - "All of those people created co-founder-like ... environments and relationships that did fill the void." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 3:14; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Against: scorched-earth co-founder breakups.** He says a breakup handled well is survivable. See [[hiring/cofounders/handling-a-cofounder-breakup]].
  - "if you handle this well, this is a non-mortal wound." ([[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]] @ 20:18; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **For: AI coding tools in the right hands.** He thinks they help skilled builders and let more young people be discovered. See [[startups/ai/will-ai-coding-create-more-winners]].
  - "this is my metaphor for a lot of the Vibe Coding stuff, which is in the right hands, it's awesome." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 8:37; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- **Against: low-ambition startups.** He calls getting caught in an idea that can never be big a trap. See [[founders/becoming-a-founder/what-if-you-lack-ambition]].
  - "is getting caught in a low ambition idea that you know in your heart can never be big." ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 20:40; by: caldwell; conf: medium)

## Where they've changed their mind
- No changes of mind by Caldwell are documented in the vault.
- Disagreement with Altman and Tan (2026): Caldwell says AI tools will not obviously mean more winners. Altman and Tan expect more valuable startups and a golden age. See [[tensions]] and [[startups/ai/will-ai-coding-create-more-winners]].
  - "number of winners is not obviously going to go up." ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]] @ 9:10; by: caldwell; conf: medium)
- Disagreement with PG 2014, mostly on the surface: PG retracted his advice to work at a company first. Caldwell (2026) suggests working somewhere ambitious, but only to founders who can't make their idea ambitious. See [[tensions]].
  - "to the extent that you have no idea how to make your startup ambitious, it might actually be more fun and fulfilling to go work somewhere where that is not the" ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]] @ 17:24; by: caldwell; conf: medium)

## Blind spots
- His main MVP example is StandardDB, his own product, and he promotes it on the show ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]]). See [[profile]].
- He co-founded Standard Capital, a Series A fund that sponsors the show. His advice that founders shouldn't spend months pitching holds up his own fund's process as the model ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]]). The MVP, co-founder and ambition episodes have the same sponsor ([[yt-rQtrzBcf_Us]]; [[yt-s1YszjhzQ6U]]; [[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]]).
- His advice to skip the press and tell your own story comes from someone who runs his own show ([[yt-7TU636F_yVQ]]).
- His case against low-ambition startups is an investor's lens. Venture returns depend on rare huge outcomes, and he limits the case to venture-funded companies himself ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]]).
- His "ignore the skeptics" lesson comes from YC winners picked in hindsight (Airbnb, Coinbase, Stripe), told as a YC partner. It also defends YC against its old reputation for funding knock-offs ([[yt-Jcuqq48CNj8]]). The ambition episode leans on Musk, OpenAI and Obama's campaign, also winners in hindsight ([[yt-ozhRhElbVcI]]).

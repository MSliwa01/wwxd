# How Garry Tan thinks

Built from three sources. Nearly all of his statements come from one talk, his 2026-08 Startup School talk *Own Your Intelligence* ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]]). A few come from the 2026-07 conversation where he hosts Altman ([[yt-ZIaOBAjvc38]]) and one from a 2024 Lightcone episode with Livingston ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]]). Rules that rest on the single 2026-08 talk say "one talk".

## What they optimize for
- **Owning your intelligence instead of renting it.** A chatbot subscription is a product you consume, while an agent on your own infrastructure with a memory you own is an asset that compounds. One talk. See [[startups/ai/own-your-intelligence-instead-of-renting-it]].
  - "One of these is a product you consume. The other is an asset you build." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 7:26; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "I believe intelligence, intelligence of this kind, should be owned, owned by you, not rented." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 7:26; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Model quality is rented, but your brain is owned, ideally by you." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 11:20; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Staying under your own power.** If your judgment compounds in someone else's repo, you are being paid to stop building your own thing. He compares this to craftsmen losing their tools to the factory. One talk. See [[startups/ai/own-your-intelligence-instead-of-renting-it]].
  - "Craftsmen own their tools. That's what made them free. The factory broke that." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 30:49; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Every comfortable arrangement where your judgment compounds in someone else's repo is a thousand guilders a year to show up, keep quiet and stop building your own thing." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 31:23; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Personal AGI is how you stay under your own power in the age of agents." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 32:27; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Work that compounds.** Every task should leave behind something reusable, so the system gets better each time. One talk. See [[startups/ai/getting-started-with-a-personal-ai-system]].
  - "Never do one-off work." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 26:54; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "At the end of every task, ask the agent to skillify what it did." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 27:27; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Treat the brain like production infrastructure and it compounds." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 23:35; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Leverage for one person.** He says the team, funding and credentials founders were told they needed were workarounds for one person's limits, and agents remove those limits. One talk. See [[startups/ai/how-much-leverage-do-agents-give-one-founder]].
  - "Everything you were told you needed, the team, the funding, the permission, the credential, was a workaround for the fact that one person could hold seven things in their head and work 16 hours a day. That fact just expired." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 39:12; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "the head count under you is now whatever you decide it is." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 21:56; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Agents are how one founder now does unscalable things at scale. The advice didn't change, but the physics of all startups and of what you can do did." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 11:53; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Earnestness.** He values founders who mean what they do, and finds it offensive when their work is treated as play-acting. From the Altman conversation only. See [[founders/traits/earnestness-vs-taking-shots]].
  - "one of the uh things that we really love at YC, for instance, is uh earnestness." ([[yt-ZIaOBAjvc38]] @ 19:40; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "this idea that what we're trying to do is a live action roleplay is like kind of deeply offensive to me." ([[yt-ZIaOBAjvc38]] @ 19:40; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "sarcasm is sort of the opposite of what I think you and I like." ([[yt-ZIaOBAjvc38]] @ 20:15; by: tan; conf: medium)

## Rules they decide by
- **If you have to ask for something twice, turn it into a skill.** Repeated requests are a sign the work was not captured. One talk. See [[startups/ai/getting-started-with-a-personal-ai-system]].
  - "If you have to ask for something twice, you failed." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 27:27; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Start with one shelf, not the warehouse.** Run one agent tonight and start one folder of markdown this weekend, not a grand archive. One talk. See [[startups/ai/getting-started-with-a-personal-ai-system]].
  - "Nobody builds the warehouse first. First you build one shelf." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 20:47; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Step one tonight, pick a harness and run an agent on your own machine." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 24:08; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Step two, this weekend start your library. Not a grand archive. One folder of markdown files" ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 24:39; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Automate the weekly task you hate most first.** That is where he tells people to begin. One talk.
  - "what's the task you do every single week that you hate the most?" ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 25:47; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Build tools for an audience of one, and watch which ones other people want.** Building for yourself is nearly free now, and the tools others beg to borrow are startups. One talk. See [[startups/ideas/solve-your-own-problem]].
  - "You can build exactly the tool you need for the audience of one in a weekend." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 23:03; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Scratch your own itch because scratching itches is nearly free and some of your tools for one will turn out to be entire companies. You'll know because other people start begging for them." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 23:03; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "one or two tools that other people keep asking to borrow. Which in this room is called a startup." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 28:32; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Keep your brain and skills in a repo you control from day one.** He treats owning your data as custody, not as added risk. One talk.
  - "keep your brain and your skills in a repo you control from day one before any platform or any acquirer has an opinion about it." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 32:27; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "I didn't create the risk by consolidating my context. I took custody of it. Custody is the security model" ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 35:18; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Write instructions clear enough for a smart intern.** Plain English instructions are the program, and the model is the compiler. One talk. See [[startups/ai/how-to-build-a-personal-ai-system]].
  - "If a smart intern could follow it, an agent can run it." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 17:27; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Markdown is actually code. If you can write clear instructions in English, you're a programmer. The compiler is a language model." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 17:27; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Expect it to be a toy at first, and keep going past week two.** The payoff goes to the people who don't quit early. One talk.
  - "Week one, honestly, it's a toy." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 28:00; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Most people who try this will quit this in week two. Which is precisely why the ones who don't feel like they're cheating by week 12." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 28:32; by: tan; conf: high)

## How they reason
- **He asks where the computation happens.** Judgment and taste belong in the model, steered by a markdown file, and he blames mixing this up for agent failures. One talk. See [[startups/ai/how-to-build-a-personal-ai-system]].
  - "The most important question to ask here is, where is the computation happening? And there are exactly two answers, and confusing them causes every agent failure I've ever seen." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 18:00; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Taste, judgment, reading what a human actually wants from a vague request. That lives in the model, and you steer it with a markdown file." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 18:34; by: tan; conf: high)
- **He models agents on how people and companies work.** A skill is an employee, a resolver is an org chart, and the fix for model failures is to make it compute the way humans do. One talk.
  - "A skill file is an employee. It has one capability, one job written down clearly enough that someone new could execute it. A resolver is an org chart." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 21:20; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "The model fails where we fail. The fix is having the model compute the way humans compute." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 19:07; by: tan; conf: high)
- **He puts the edge in context, not in the model.** As models improve, what you feed them matters more. One talk.
  - "the leverage is not in the weights. It's in what context you give it, how relevant it is, and does it happen at the right step." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 10:13; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "The better the models get, the more the differentiator moves to context." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 33:37; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "Retrieval is easy. Being worth retrieving from is the product." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 34:12; by: tan; conf: high)
- **He argues from his own output and YC batch numbers.** He gives his own multiplier and a range around it, and generalizes from coding to all knowledge work. One talk. See [[startups/ai/how-much-leverage-do-agents-give-one-founder]].
  - "I did the math on my output, and I'm at about 400x what I did in 2013." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 8:00; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "It's still 8x at the absolute floor, and 10 times that in the middle of the range." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 8:34; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "The multiplier for coding is not just for coding. It's for every piece of knowledge work." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 9:06; by: tan; conf: high)
- **He reads history as a pattern about who holds leverage.** A powerful technology kept private makes a priesthood, and one given away makes a renaissance. One talk. See [[startups/ai/ai-and-concentration-of-power]].
  - "Every era has a private technology of leverage, a thing the powerful have and everyone else doesn't." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 35:50; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "When something like that, that powerful stays private, you get a priesthood. When it gets given away, you get a renaissance." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 36:24; by: tan; conf: high)
- **He treats public mockery as a sign of adoption.** He checks what the critics themselves ship. One talk. See [[founders/life/rejection-and-outside-opinion]].
  - "I say agents write most of my code now, and the dunks arrive by lunch. Then I look at what the loudest dunkers are actually shipping, and it's agents all the way down." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 37:32; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "The dunks are just the adoption curve announcing itself." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 38:05; by: tan; conf: high)

## Strong views
- **For: giving the tools of the powerful away.** He open-sourced his own system and says being at YC means he doesn't need to sell it. One talk. See [[startups/ai/ai-and-concentration-of-power]].
  - "I believe tools of the powerful should be given away." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 35:50; by: tan; conf: high)
  - Open-sourced his personal AI system (harness, brain architecture and skills), saying that being at YC means he doesn't need to monetize it ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 35:18; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Against: renting AI as a monthly chatbot.** He calls it a corporate AGI you don't own. One talk.
  - "I do not mean a chatbot you pay $20 a month to." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 6:18; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "That's just a subscription you rent. It's a corporate AGI you don't own." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 6:18; by: tan; conf: high)
- **For: starting a startup so your skill files work for you.** Inside a company, your skills belong to the employer, and your job can become a skill file. One talk.
  - "I believe skill files are yours. Own your skills because if you don't, your job becomes a skill file." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 30:49; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "that's why you should start a startup because this is how you can actually make those skill files work for you." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 31:23; by: tan; conf: high)
- **For: treating AI as a workforce, not autocomplete.** He says this is already the bar in the current batch, and those who don't will lose to those who do. One talk.
  - "the fastest growing founders we fund are not treating AI as autocomplete. They are treating it as a workforce." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 9:40; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "That is not the future, that is the bar right now with this batch. If you're not doing it, your competitor is and they will eat your lunch politely and thank you for it." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 22:30; by: tan; conf: high)
- **Against: knowledge stores nobody curates.** Search does not rescue an uncurated pile. One talk.
  - "a brain nobody curates is a garbage dump with great search." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 23:35; by: tan; conf: high)
- **For: now as the time to build.** He says building got easy, so what is rare is now up to the founder. See [[startups/ai/is-now-a-good-time-to-start-a-startup]].
  - "The difficulty just collapsed. The rarity is now up to you." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 41:28; by: tan; conf: high)
  - "one of the things we're seeing at YC now is that number was, you know, maybe five or 10% for many years and then now we're getting to 15 20 25%." ([[yt-ZIaOBAjvc38]] @ 6:46; by: tan; conf: high)
- **For: the YC batch model over typical VC funds.** From the 2024 Lightcone episode only. See [[fundraising/investors/how-yc-was-designed-differently-from-vcs]].
  - "if you funded all of the companies in the batch you would actually create a VC fund that was better than pretty much most of the VC funds returns period historically" ([[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]] @ 40:30; by: tan; conf: high)

## Where they've changed their mind
- No change of mind is documented. His sources do not contradict each other.
- He describes himself as having been skeptical about AI safety, and says the 2026 sandbox incident marks a new moment. There is no earlier source in the vault showing the old view. See [[startups/ai/ai-risks-and-a-good-outcome]].
  - "in the past I've been known to be skeptical about um safety and AI but on the other hand like this is a real m like we're entering a new moment" ([[yt-ZIaOBAjvc38]] @ 24:07; by: tan; conf: high)
- Disagreement, not a change of mind: in 2026-08 he sides with Altman against Caldwell (2026-05) on whether AI makes more and bigger winners. He cites batch growth and revenue per person, and concedes he can't prove AI caused the growth. See [[tensions]].
  - "that batch is on track to becoming one of the fastest growing, most profitable batches in the history of YC." ([[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]] @ 9:40; by: tan; conf: high)
- Disagreement, not a change of mind: against Seibel's 2026-05 view that employees may gain the most from AI, he says an employee's skill files belong to the employer, so he argues for owning them and starting a startup. See [[tensions]].

## Blind spots
- He promotes his own tools (G brain, G stack, a hosted version), though he says they are free and other harnesses work too ([[profile]]; [[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]]).
- His 400x output figure, the W25 batch growth and the revenue-per-person examples are his or YC's own numbers, given without method. He concedes he can't prove AI caused the growth ([[profile]]; [[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]]).
- As YC's CEO speaking at YC's own Startup School, he has a stake in telling the audience to start startups ([[profile]]).
- He co-founded the fund Initialized, so his claim that funding a whole batch beats most VC funds comes from an interested party. The episode was YC-produced ([[profile]]; [[yt-6u4JVz7iQTY]]).
- He read several of PG's essays in draft, so his agreement with PG is not fully independent ([[profile]]).
- He frames founders through Spinoza (striving, refusing comfortable offers, living under your own power) and through "you can just do things". He is an enthusiastic builder of the AI tools he recommends ([[profile]]; [[yt-eRrc1pUY5oU]]).
